PART 1 — GENERAL UX OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Complaint, Review and Suggestion drawers should no longer look like raw backend/admin forms.

They should look like professional CRM records.

Every drawer should clearly answer:

1. What is this record?
2. Which client/order does it belong to?
3. What happened?
4. What is its current status?
5. What does that status mean?
6. Who performed each action?
7. When did each action happen?
8. Is there evidence/screenshot?
9. What action is required next?
10. What was the final outcome?

Use simple professional English.

Avoid:
- technical database wording
- confusing status names
- vague labels
- backend-style history entries
- unexplained workflow states

==================================================
FINAL PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
==================================================

The CRM must tell a HUMAN STORY.

Not a database story.

When someone opens a Complaint they should understand:

What the client complained about.
Who the complaint was against.
Whether management confirmed or dismissed it.
Why it was dismissed if dismissed.
How it was resolved if confirmed.
Whether a refund happened.
Who performed each action.
When it happened.
What evidence exists.
What happened to the related Order.

When someone opens a Review they should immediately understand:

What the client said.
What rating they gave.
Which public feedback channels were received.
Who recorded it.
What evidence exists.

When someone opens a Suggestion they should understand:

What was suggested.
Who recorded it.
Whether management implemented or rejected it.
Why where applicable.

All three systems must use:

professional wording
simple language
consistent UI
clear history
clear evidence
clear actions
clear role permissions

DO NOT make the CRM technically complicated for the employee.

Make the backend strong and the frontend simple.
